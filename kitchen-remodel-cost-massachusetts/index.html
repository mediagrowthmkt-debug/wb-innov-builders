Kitchen Remodel Cost in Massachusetts: What Changes the Price

Kitchen2026-08-27· Iago Maciel· Licensed & Insured · HIC #212869 · OSHA Certified

The kitchen is the most expensive room to remodel, and the one where homeowners get the most conflicting numbers. Ask around Wakefield or anywhere in Greater Boston and you will hear everything from $25,000 to $150,000, all for "a kitchen remodel." So which is it? The honest answer: the price depends almost entirely on decisions you have not made yet.

At Innov Builders, we build kitchens across Massachusetts, and we believe you deserve to understand the math before you sign anything. This guide explains what a kitchen remodel really costs here, and, more usefully, exactly what changes the price, so you can shape the budget instead of being surprised by it.

Kitchen remodel cost ranges in Massachusetts

Treat these as planning ranges for a licensed, permitted remodel in the Greater Boston area, not fixed quotes. The right number for your kitchen depends on the choices below.

LevelTypical budget rangeWhat it generally includes
Minor / mid refresh$25,000 – $45,000New cabinets or refacing, countertops, appliances, backsplash, flooring; same layout
Full mid-range remodel$50,000 – $85,000New custom cabinetry, quartz counters, island, updated plumbing and electrical, lighting, flooring
High-end / layout change$90,000 – $150,000+Moved walls, relocated plumbing, premium cabinetry and appliances, structural work, high-end finishes

A kitchen spans a huge range because it involves more trades than any other room: carpentry, cabinetry, countertops, plumbing, electrical, flooring, tile and often structural work. Each one is a lever on the final price.

What actually changes the price of a kitchen remodel

When we scope a kitchen in the Wakefield area, these are the factors that move the number the most. Knowing them is how you get the kitchen you want at a price you can live with.

1. Cabinets, the biggest single line item

Cabinetry is usually the largest cost in a kitchen, often 30 to 40 percent of the budget. Your choice here swings the total more than anything else:

  • Cabinet refacing or refinishing, the most economical, keeps the boxes and updates the doors and finish.
  • Stock or semi-custom cabinets, a middle path with good quality and set sizes.
  • Fully custom cabinetry, built to fit your exact space and style, the premium option, and where our finish carpenters shine.

If your layout works and your boxes are solid, refacing can transform the kitchen for a fraction of full replacement. We will tell you honestly which path fits your kitchen.

2. Countertops and materials

Laminate is budget-friendly. Quartz is the popular mid-to-premium choice, durable, low-maintenance and beautiful. Granite and natural stone vary widely by slab. Countertop cost also depends on square footage and how many seams and cutouts the layout requires.

3. Are you changing the layout?

Just like a bathroom, keeping the plumbing and the footprint saves the most money. The moment you move the sink, relocate the range, take out a wall or open the kitchen to the living room, you add plumbing, electrical and often structural work, plus engineering and permits. A great new look does not always require a new layout, and we will show you what is possible without moving everything.

4. Appliances

This is entirely your call, and the range is enormous. A solid appliance package versus a professional-grade suite can differ by tens of thousands of dollars. Appliances are the easiest place to control your budget up or down without affecting the quality of the construction.

5. Plumbing and electrical

Modern kitchens demand more: dedicated circuits, under-cabinet and recessed lighting, a pot filler, an island with power, better ventilation. Older Massachusetts homes often need electrical panel upgrades to support it all. These are licensed-trade costs that belong in the budget from the start.

6. Flooring, backsplash and finishes

Tile, hardwood and luxury vinyl each carry different price points, and the backsplash can range from a simple subway tile to an intricate feature wall. These finishes are where personality lives, and where you can dial spending to match your budget.

7. What is behind the walls (and permits)

Older Greater Boston homes hide outdated wiring, worn plumbing and the occasional structural surprise. A realistic budget includes a contingency for this. And in Massachusetts, kitchen remodels that touch plumbing, electrical or structure require permits and inspections, which Innov Builders handles for you as your licensed contractor.

Does a kitchen remodel pay off in Massachusetts?

The kitchen is the room buyers care about most, so a smart remodel is one of the strongest home investments in the Greater Boston market. A dated kitchen is often the single biggest reason a home lingers on the market or sells below its potential. A fresh, functional kitchen removes that objection and lifts the perceived value of the entire house.

As with any renovation, the return depends on restraint. A mid-range remodel with quality cabinetry, quartz counters and timeless finishes tends to recover a healthy share of its cost while giving you years of daily use. Ultra-luxury choices in a modest home return less of their premium. For most Wakefield-area homeowners, the value sweet spot is a well-built mid-range kitchen that fixes the layout's pain points and looks clean and current, not the most expensive suite of appliances on the market.

How long does a kitchen remodel take?

A kitchen is a bigger undertaking than a bathroom, and the timeline reflects it. Most full kitchen remodels in the Wakefield area run six to ten weeks of active construction, plus several weeks beforehand for design and material selections, especially cabinets, which carry the longest lead times. Layout changes, structural work and custom cabinetry extend that window.

The single biggest schedule risk is cabinetry lead time. Custom and semi-custom cabinets can take many weeks to build and ship, so we finalize the cabinet order early and build the rest of the schedule around it. As with any remodel, the projects that run long are almost always the ones where selections were not locked in before demolition began.

Choosing cabinets: the decision that shapes everything

Because cabinets drive both cost and timeline, this is the decision to get right first. A quick guide:

  • Refacing or refinishing makes sense when your cabinet boxes are structurally sound and the layout works. It is the fastest and most affordable path to a new look.
  • Stock cabinets come in set sizes and finishes, good value when they fit your space well.
  • Semi-custom cabinets offer more sizes, finishes and features, a strong balance of cost and flexibility.
  • Fully custom cabinetry is built to your exact dimensions and style, ideal for awkward older-home layouts and where you want a truly bespoke result.

Our finish carpenters install every cabinet level, plumb and solid, with clean reveals and tight seams, because even the finest cabinets look cheap if they are hung poorly. The quality of the install is as important as the quality of the box.

How to get the kitchen you want for less

Spending well matters as much as the total. A few strategies we share with our clients:

  • Keep the layout, upgrade the surfaces. New cabinets, counters and finishes on the existing footprint deliver a stunning result for far less.
  • Consider refacing if your cabinet boxes are in good shape.
  • Choose quartz over rare stone for the best mix of beauty, durability and value.
  • Control the appliance budget, it is the easiest lever with no impact on construction quality.
  • Do it once, do it right. Bundling the work with one accountable crew beats years of piecemeal fixes.

Where the money goes in a typical kitchen

To make the ranges concrete, here is roughly how a mid-range Massachusetts kitchen remodel tends to break down. Your project will vary, but the proportions show why the total lands where it does:

CategoryApprox. share of budget
Cabinetry30–40%
Countertops10–15%
Appliances10–20%
Labor, installation & project management15–20%
Plumbing & electrical8–12%
Flooring, backsplash & finishes7–12%
Permits & contingency5–10%

This is why two "kitchen quotes" can be so far apart, a change in the cabinet tier or the appliance package alone can move the total by tens of thousands of dollars. When you understand the breakdown, you can see exactly which levers to pull to hit your budget.

Common kitchen remodel budget mistakes

We see the same avoidable mistakes cost homeowners money again and again:

  • Forgetting the "invisible" costs. Demolition, disposal, permits, and updating old plumbing and wiring are real line items, not afterthoughts.
  • No contingency. In older Greater Boston homes, opening the walls reveals surprises. A 10 to 15 percent buffer keeps a surprise from becoming a crisis.
  • Chasing the lowest bid. A quote far below the others usually means something is missing, cheaper cabinets, skipped permits, or a low allowance you will blow past the moment you go shopping.
  • Changing your mind mid-project. Selection changes after work starts trigger change orders and delays. Lock in your choices before demo.

A good contractor helps you avoid all four by building a complete, honest budget from the start.

Why the right contractor protects your kitchen budget

A kitchen remodel goes over budget for one of two reasons: hidden conditions nobody planned for, or a contractor who low-balled the quote to win the job and then piled on change orders. A good builder prevents both by scoping the project honestly, flagging likely surprises up front, and giving you a clear written estimate you can trust.
