Why it matters

What each license protects you from

Hiring an unlicensed or uninsured contractor is the most expensive shortcut a homeowner can take. Here is what you keep when the paperwork is in order.

HIC: the Guaranty Fund

Massachusetts law (Chapter 142A) requires a written contract for jobs over $1,000 and caps the deposit at one third of the price. Only homeowners who hire a registered HIC can access the state Guaranty Fund, which can reimburse up to $10,000 if a contractor fails to finish the job.

CSL: permits and inspections

Structural work needs a building permit, and the permit is pulled under a licensed Construction Supervisor. That means the town inspects the framing, the structure and the final result, and your remodel stays legal when you sell the house.

Insurance: your home and the crew

General Liability pays for accidental damage to your property. Workers' Compensation pays if a worker is injured on site. Without it, an accident at your house can turn into a claim against your own homeowner's policy.

Homeowner checklist

How to verify a contractor in Massachusetts

Four checks that take less than ten minutes and protect a project worth tens of thousands of dollars. Not sure which license your project needs? See the state HIC or CSL checklist.

Ask for the HIC number

Every registered contractor has one. Ours is HIC #212869. Look it up on the state HIC lookup and confirm the name and status.

Check the Construction Supervisor

For structural work, ask who holds the CSL and confirm it is active. That person is responsible for the permit.

Get the insurance certificate

Ask for a Certificate of Insurance sent directly by the insurance agent, showing General Liability and Workers' Compensation in date.

Read the contract

Under Massachusetts law it must be in writing for jobs over $1,000, with the HIC number, start and end dates and a deposit of no more than one third.
